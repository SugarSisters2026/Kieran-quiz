What an unfair term is

When you signed the mortgage you signed a booklet of conditions you had no part in writing. Nobody negotiates those. You take them or you get no loan. Because of that, European consumer law, part of Irish law since 19951, lets a court strike out a term in that booklet if it tilts the bargain unfairly towards the lender2.

1.VERIFIEDEuropean Communities (Unfair Terms in Consumer Contracts) Regulations 1995, S.I. No. 27 of 1995
2.VERIFIEDCouncil Directive 93/13/EEC of 5 April 1993 on unfair terms in consumer contracts
Article 3(1)

The fact that you signed it does not save it. That is the whole point of the rule. If signing saved it, the rule would protect nobody.

The thing on your loan offer

Look at the offer letter from April 2009. There is a table of important information. Item seven gives the APR, and there is a small star beside it3.

Follow that star down to the bottom of the page and it leads to a note. The note explains what the European Central Bank rate is4.

Now look at the rate you were offered. Three point seven five per cent. And the European Central Bank rate that month was also three point seven five per cent5. The same number.

5.PULL IT FIRSTECB Main Refinancing Operations rate, April 2009
stated as 3.75 per cent — CONFIRM from the ECB rate history before this is said in court

So you have a star beside your rate, pointing at the European Central Bank, and the two figures are identical. Any ordinary person reading that would take it that the two were connected.

They were not connected at all. The rate was the bank's own rate, which the bank could move whenever it liked.

One word you must never use

Do not call this a tracker. It was not a tracker mortgage, the judge in the Circuit Court found that it was not6, and she was right about it.

6.IN THE PAPERSJudgment of Her Honour Judge Jacqueline Linnane, 27 January 2020, Circuit Court record 2015/4877
finding that it clearly was not a tracker mortgage and that no evidence was adduced in support

If you say the word tracker, the other side will read out that finding and the whole argument dies in front of you.

What you are saying is different. You are not claiming the rate was tied to the European Central Bank. You are saying the paperwork was written in a way that made it look as though it was. That is a complaint about the document being misleading, not about what the rate actually was.

What the conditions actually say about the rate

The offer says it is made under the conditions in the booklet dated February 20087. The clause dealing with the interest rate is clause three point three8.

7.IN THE PAPERSBank of Scotland (Ireland) Home Loans Terms and Conditions, February 2008
incorporated by the borrowers' signed acceptance of the offer
8.IN THE PAPERSFebruary 2008 Terms and Conditions
Clause 3.3

Read it and you find it says almost nothing. It says the bank will give you notice if it changes the rate, and the change takes effect from the date in the notice.

That is the entire mechanism. There is no mention of the European Central Bank. There is no benchmark of any kind. There is no limit and no list of reasons. Nothing you could ever use to check whether a change was justified.

So the paper you signed points at an outside benchmark, and the paper that actually controls the rate has no benchmark in it at all. The two documents contradict each other.

The gate, and this is the important bit

There is an exception in the law, and the other side will reach for it. It says a court cannot assess the fairness of a term that is about the main subject matter of the contract9. The price, the core of the bargain.

9.VERIFIEDCouncil Directive 93/13/EEC
Article 4(2)

But that exception comes with a condition attached, and it is the condition that decides your case. The exception only applies if the term is written in plain and understandable language10.

10.PULL IT FIRSTAndriciuc v Banca Romaneasca SA, Case C-186/16, CJEU, 20 September 2017
the Article 4(2) exclusion applies only where the term is in plain intelligible language

Think of it as a gate. To reach the shelter of that exception, a term has to get through the gate of being clearly written. If it cannot get through the gate, the court is entitled to look at whether it is fair11.

11.PULL IT FIRSTKasler v OTP Jelzalogbank Zrt, Case C-26/13, CJEU, 30 April 2014
plain intelligible language; variation without objective criteria

A star on one page pointing at a benchmark that does not appear anywhere in the conditions is not plain and understandable language. So the term does not get through the gate.

What the Circuit Court judge did, and what she did not do

Get this one wrong and you will be corrected in open court. The judge in 2020 did look at unfair terms. It is on the last page of her judgment12. Never say no court has looked at this, because it is not true and they will show the judge the page.

12.IN THE PAPERSLinnane J judgment, 27 January 2020
final page — the 1995 Regulations and Directive 93/13; main subject matter held to be the obligation to make repayments

What she decided was that the term was about the main subject matter of the contract, so the exception applied. And that in any event it was the failure to repay that gave the right to enforce.

What she never did was ask the other question. She never asked whether the term was written in plain and understandable language. She never went to the gate.

So your point is not that she was wrong. Your point is that the question was never reached, and that this court, hearing the case fresh, has to reach it13.

13.VERIFIEDTanager DAC and Pepper Finance Corporation (Ireland) DAC v Doyle [2025] IEHC 275, Roberts J, 13 May 2025
paras 22, 23 and 29 — the appeal proceeds by way of rehearing on the evidence given in the Circuit Court, s.37(2) Courts of Justice Act 1936

The other three clauses, briefly

Clause five point two d lets them charge interest on your arrears at the same rate as the loan itself14. European courts have held that piling the full rate onto arrears puts a disproportionate burden on somebody already in difficulty15, and that interest properly runs on the credit actually advanced16.

Clause seven point four says the bank's own certificate is conclusive proof of what you owe17. Such clauses are treated as prima facie unfair18. That is worth attacking here for a particular reason: the arrears figure in this case had to be adjusted once already, by about twenty four thousand euro19. Why this is not too late

In consumer cases the duty to check whether a term is unfair sits on the court itself, not on you. The judge is obliged to look at it even if nobody raises it. Our Supreme Court has called that a matter of public policy22, and it applies right through to enforcement, even after judgment23.

22.PULL IT FIRSTCannon [2020] IESC 2, O'Malley J
para 122 — the own-motion obligation as a matter of public policy
23.PULL IT FIRSTColeman [2025] IEHC 747, Stack J
the own-motion duty at the enforcement stage, post-judgment

So the answer to why did you not say this years ago is that it would have made no difference. The obligation was on the court the whole time.

What you are asking for

Not that the mortgage disappears. Not that you never owed the money.

You are asking the judge to send the case to a full trial so that the fairness of these terms can be properly examined, with documents and witnesses, instead of being decided on affidavits. A court cannot conduct that assessment without the contractual documents in front of it24.

24.PULL IT FIRSTKenehan [2017] IEHC 604, Barrett J
possession refused where the contractual documents were not before the court

The bar for that is low. You do not have to prove the terms are unfair. You have to show there is a real argument worth testing25.

25.VERIFIEDTanager DAC and Pepper v Doyle [2025] IEHC 275, Roberts J
paras 28, 31 and 32 — a relatively low bar of an arguable defence; the burden of proof remains on the respondents

Earlier this year another judge sent a possession case to full trial on exactly these grounds26. It is not a long shot.

If you remember nothing else, remember these five

  1. Signing the conditions does not save an unfair term. That is the point of the rule.
  2. The star beside the APR points at the European Central Bank rate. The conditions never mention it.
  3. A core term escapes scrutiny only if it is plainly written. That is the gate, and this one does not get through it.
  4. The judge in 2020 did look at unfair terms, but she never asked the plain language question. Never say no court looked.
  5. Never say the word tracker.

Read it again tomorrow. It gets shorter every time.
